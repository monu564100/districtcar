Gumla District Tourism & Hidden Gems Guide 2026: Anjan Dham, Palkot, Tanginath & Basudevkona

Spiritual Hubs: Anjan Dham (Birthplace of Hanuman Ji), Tanginath Dham (Ancient Rust-Proof Trident), Basudevkona.

Nature & Wildlife: Palkot Wildlife Sanctuary, prehistoric megalithic caves, and Nagfeni royal palace ruins.

Resting in the lap of lush Sal jungles and granite hills, Gumla district is one of Jharkhand’s most spiritually potent and archaeologically intriguing regions. Derived from the Mundari word Gaw-Mela (cattle fair), Gumla remains largely off the conventional commercial tourist radar, preserving an aura of tranquil mysticism, prehistoric cave shelters, and epic Ramayana legends.

For travelers seeking raw authenticity, uncrowded sacred shrines, and scenic plateau drives, here is the complete 2026 guide to Gumla's hidden treasures.

Top Hidden Gems to Explore in Gumla District

1. Anjan Dham — The Sacred Birthplace of Lord Hanuman

Situated 21 km from Gumla town, Anjan Dham is revered by millions of devotees across India. According to ancient Puranic belief and local oral tradition, this serene hilltop village was the abode of Maharishi Gautama's daughter, Mata Anjani, where Lord Hanuman was born in a secluded mountain cave.

A steep stone flight leads to the sacred cave atop the hill, where an ancient idol depicting Mata Anjani seated with child Hanuman in her lap is worshipped. Near the base of the hill lies Chakradhari Shivling and hundreds of natural rock shelters historically used by meditating rishis.

2. Palkot Wildlife Sanctuary & The King Sugriva Cave

Located 25 km south of Gumla along the Simdega border, Palkot is renowned for its labyrinth of gigantic granite boulders, hidden water springs, and prehistoric rock art. Folklore links Palkot to Kishkindha of the Ramayana era, specifically the Sugriva Guha where King Sugriva hid from his brother Vali.

The sanctuary is rich in biodiversity, sheltering leopards, sloth bears, wild boars, and diverse bird species across its dry deciduous forest. Cool springs like Sheetalpur flow perennial chilled water through subterranean granite fissures even in the peak of summer.

3. Tanginath Dham — The Mystery of the Rust-Proof Trident

Perched in the dense forest of Dumri block near the Chhattisgarh border, Tanginath Dham is one of the most enigmatic archaeological sites in eastern India. Spread over a forested hillock are hundreds of ancient stone Shiva lingams, Deva-Devi statues, and Surya sculptures dating from the 7th to 10th century.

In the center of this sacred complex stands a giant iron Trishul (Trident) thrust into the earth, which local legends attribute to Lord Parashurama discarding his battle axe and trident to perform deep penance. Despite being exposed to rain and sun for over a thousand years, the trident shows zero signs of rust or oxidation—an ancient metallurgical marvel.

4. Nagfeni Palace Ruins & Cobra Stone

Located near Sisai on the Ranchi-Gumla highway, Nagfeni is named after a natural rock formation resembling the raised hood of a cobra (Nag-phani). Here, the ancient Nagvanshi kings of Chotanagpur built a grand stone fort and palace around 1704 AD. Today, the atmospheric stone ruins and carvings of kings and queens overlook the Koel river valley.

5. Basudevkona

Situated 3 km from Gumla town, Basudevkona is an ancient seat of Vaishnavite devotion, hosting stone shrines dedicated to Lord Krishna (Basudeva) carved during the medieval era.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Is mobile network connectivity available at Tanginath and Anjan Dham?

Anjan Dham and Gumla town have strong 4G/5G mobile connectivity across all networks. Tanginath Dham, being deep in the forested hills, has intermittent signal, so downloading offline maps or traveling with a verified District Car local driver is recommended.

Are there good hotels to stay in Gumla?

Gumla town offers several comfortable budget and mid-range family hotels, clean dhabas, and government tourism inspection bungalows near Palkot.

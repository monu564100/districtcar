⚡ Betla National Park Key Facts

Location: Latehar and Palamu districts, Jharkhand.

Distance from Ranchi: 170 km via NH39 (4 hours by cab).

Key Wildlife: Wild Asian elephants, gaur (Indian bison), chital deer, sloth bears, sambar, and leopards.

Historical Monument: 16th-century Palamu Forts of the Chero Kings hidden deep in the forest.

Spanning over 226 square kilometers of dense Sal, teak, and bamboo forests, Betla National Park was one of the earliest national parks in India to be declared under Project Tiger in 1974. Today, it remains an extraordinary wildlife haven where herds of wild elephants graze peacefully beside historic royal ruins.

Exploring Palamu Forts (पुराना और नया किला)

Located just 5 km inside the forest from the main gate, the twin forts of the Chero rulers—King Medini Ray—date back to the 16th and 17th centuries. Rising dramatically above the Auranga River, the ancient Islamic and Hindu architectural fusion, including the famous carved Nagpuri Gate, is a photographer's dream.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does Betla National Park remain closed?

The park is usually closed during monsoon breeding season from July 1 to September 30. The best visiting window is from October to April.
